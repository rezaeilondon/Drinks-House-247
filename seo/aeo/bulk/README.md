# Duplicate Product schema — remove hand-written JSON-LD from product descriptions

## The defect

Affected product pages carry **two competing `Product` entities**:

1. **Hand-written**, pasted into the product description. Hardcoded price, hardcoded
   `"availability": "InStock"`, no `@id`, no variant handling.
2. **Generated by Smart SEO** (`snippets/smartseo.product.jsonld.liquid`) on every
   product template, built at request time from `variant.price` and `variant.available`,
   with one `Offer` per variant plus `sku`, `mpn`, `gtin`, `weight` and `priceValidUntil`.

The second is strictly better and cannot go stale. The first drifts the moment a price
changes — and 18 of the 27 products checked on 2026-09-17 had already drifted:

| Direction | Count | Worst case |
|---|---|---|
| JSON-LD **higher** than the real price | 8 | The Malt Collector's Hamper — advertised £375, actually £290 |
| JSON-LD **lower** than the real price | 10 | Dom Pérignon 2008 — advertised £366, actually £395 |

Both directions are damaging, for different reasons. Quoting **high** loses the sale:
Don Julio 1942 was being advertised to machines at £225 against a real £165. Quoting
**low** is the legally riskier one — displaying a price you do not honour is a
misleading action under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008.

This now matters more than it did. The store's `robots.txt` directs AI shopping agents to
read catalog and cart through the **UCP/MCP endpoint** at `/api/ucp/mcp`, which serves
the real variant price. An agent that also reads the page HTML gets the frozen one. Two
machine-readable channels on the same store, disagreeing about price.

The hardcoded `"availability": "InStock"` is the quieter half of the same bug: an
out-of-stock product still tells a crawler it is in stock.

## The fix

**Delete the hand-written block.** Do not update the number — that only resets the clock
until the next price change. `strip_product_jsonld.py` removes a JSON-LD block only when
it parses as JSON *and* declares `"@type": "Product"`. Anything unparseable, or of any
other type, is left byte-for-byte alone.

## Scope

`products-strip-product-jsonld.csv` covers the **27 products pulled fresh on 2026-09-17**,
which includes all 18 known price mismatches. Importing it resolves every currently-known
contradiction.

**The full job is larger: 1,357 of 1,449 products carry inline JSON-LD.** The remaining
~1,330 are not yet drifted but are sitting on the same fault. To do them all, re-pull
every product description fresh and run the script over it — do not reuse an older
snapshot, because applying a stale body would silently revert unrelated edits.

## Two things this deliberately does NOT fix

Found while reading `dom-perignon-vintage-2008-75cl`, and left alone because they are the
owner's data to confirm, not mine to guess at:

- **A hardcoded review count in visible HTML** — "4.8 on Google · 223 reviews". If it was
  accurate when written it is drifting now, and unlike the JSON-LD there is no app
  generating a live replacement.
- **Hardcoded cross-sell prices** — the chocolate pairing cards quote £28.00 and £29.00.
  Same failure mode as the JSON-LD prices, in visible copy.

Both are worth a separate sweep across all product descriptions.
